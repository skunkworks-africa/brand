Patterns & experiences / 1.0

Turn interest into a useful conversation.

A practical pattern for landing pages that collect enquiries, qualify needs and explain what happens next.

Start with an outcome

A lead-capture page connects a specific offer to a clear next action. Use this pattern for enquiries, assessments, event registrations and requests for a quotation. Adapt the fields, privacy notice and follow-up to the actual service and audience.

Decide who the page serves, what the person receives, who follows up, and what information is needed before choosing components. Use the form controls guidance for labels, hints and validation.

A complete page, at a glance

01 / Offer

A clear promise

Describe the service, intended audience and useful outcome. Set a realistic expectation.

02 / Evidence

Reasons to trust

Use verifiable evidence, approved customer references and relevant privacy information.

03 / Action

A focused form

Ask only what is needed now. Keep the primary action and required fields clear.

04 / Follow-up

What happens next

Explain the next step and provide a useful confirmation or recovery path.

    Choose the right elements

    Elements to consider for a landing page
    ElementUse it whenDesign considerations
    Headline and supporting copyAlwaysMake the offer, audience and next action clear. Avoid unsupported claims.
    Primary call to actionAlwaysUse a specific action label and a consistent destination. Keep it reachable on mobile.
    Trust and proofWhen evidence is availableUse approved, accurate customer references, certifications or case studies; check permissions and expiry.
    Privacy notice and preferencesWhen collecting informationExplain the purpose, relevant contact and privacy information. Keep optional marketing choices separate and unchecked.
    Qualification fieldsWhen needed for follow-upExplain why sensitive or detailed questions are needed; progressively ask for more where appropriate.
    Progress indicator or stepsFor longer or multi-step journeysShow progress and preserve answers when moving backwards. Let people correct entries.
    FAQ or alternate contactWhen people need reassurance or another routeAnswer real objections and provide monitored contact options without distracting from the main task.
    File uploadOnly when a document is necessaryExplain type and size limits; implement secure storage, validation and malware scanning in the service.
    Scheduling, price estimate or downloadWhen it is the promised next stepShow limitations, eligibility and any costs clearly; connect a real service before making a promise.

    Choose fields deliberately

    • Begin with a way to address and contact the person, and the specific information needed to respond. Add company, role, country, requirements, budget or timeframe only when the follow-up process uses them.
    • Mark required and optional fields, explain unfamiliar questions and support international names, telephone numbers and locations.
    • Use progressive disclosure or multiple steps when a long application is unavoidable. Do not use a progress indicator to make a short form artificially longer.
    • Keep optional marketing permission separate from the request. Record the wording, choice and time when the production service needs that record.

    Design every state

    • Initial: show the offer, expected next step, clear labels and privacy information before the person submits.
    • Validation: show specific field errors and, when appropriate, a linked error summary; retain all values.
    • Submitting: prevent accidental duplicate submissions and announce progress without removing the person’s context.
    • Success: confirm the request only after the service accepts it. Explain the next step and provide a reference or alternative contact where available.
    • Failure or offline: explain what happened, preserve entries and offer retry or another monitored channel. Do not claim success when a request fails.
    • Unavailable or ineligible: explain the constraint and offer a relevant alternative where possible.

    Connect the production journey

    • Use a secure backend with server-side validation, transport encryption, access controls, abuse protection, retention rules and monitoring. Prevent duplicate requests with an appropriate idempotency strategy.
    • Connect submissions to the appropriate CRM or service queue. Define ownership, routing, response targets, retries and an escalation process before launch.
    • Provide a privacy notice that covers the actual processing and obtain any permissions required for the intended communications. Have the relevant jurisdictional requirements reviewed.
    • Use accessible names, logical headings, keyboard navigation, visible focus, sufficient contrast, responsive layouts and clear error messages. Test with assistive technology and users.
    • Measure meaningful steps such as page view, form start, successful submission and submission error only where permitted. Respect consent settings and avoid sending names, email addresses, message text or other personal data in analytics events.
    • Test at narrow widths and with poor connectivity. Avoid unnecessary images, scripts and tracking; keep the form usable when nonessential services fail.

    Release checklist

    • The offer, audience, action and next step are accurate and easy to understand.
    • Trust claims, partner marks and testimonials are current and approved for this use.
    • Required fields have a clear purpose; the privacy notice and optional communication choices match the real process.
    • Client and server validation, error recovery, duplicate prevention and success confirmation are tested end to end.
    • CRM routing, staff ownership, follow-up and monitoring have been verified with a test submission.
    • Accessibility, mobile layout, performance and permitted analytics have been checked in the real deployment.
